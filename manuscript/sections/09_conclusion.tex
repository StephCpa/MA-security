\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Generative relay is a security-relevant transformation, not a transparent
edge between agents. It can erase literal lineage while leaving measurable
action-related content in some derived texts, and high-retention derivatives
can be delegated onward through two receivers. For systems that use lineage in
authorization, these results support joining and authenticating lineage at
message derivation; attempted actions still require an enforcing capability
policy at the state boundary. When elevation and write authority can fail together, their
excess expected mutation count is the failure covariance times the measured
endpoint-and-fixture response to elevation, under the additive single-path
assumptions stated in the supplementary appendix; that response must be
re-estimated for each fixture. The three stages were measured on separate
fixtures; closing transformation, propagation, and authorization in one
pipeline that also measures benign utility is the next step.
