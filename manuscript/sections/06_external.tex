\section{Protocol Boundaries and External Execution}
\label{sec:external}

\subsection{Distinct credentials can share one failure root}

We instantiated the common-root topology in MCP Inspector 2.4.0 at pinned
commit \texttt{edf54f5d}, using synthetic credentials and official scope
gates. Two resource entries with distinct token identifiers and scopes were
persisted in one \texttt{NodeOAuthStorage} file. Exposing an individual entry
opened only its gate; exposing the whole file opened both. Splitting the same
entries across files made a single-file exposure open only one gate. Yet
revocation remained separate in the shared-file case: clearing D changed
$(D,C)$ from $(1,1)$ to $(0,1)$, and clearing C then produced $(0,0)$.
Token count and revocation granularity therefore do not reveal compromise
covariance; deployments must enumerate shared storage, issuer sessions,
principals, processes, hosts, refresh paths, and recovery mechanisms.

This version-pinned study establishes an allowed common-root configuration in
an official debugging and conformance tool, not a production-host fault rate,
an MCP vulnerability, or the semantic $a$--$b$ bridge itself. Its role is to
show that the declared topology corresponds to a concrete credential layout.

\subsection{A2A lineage survives transport but not derivation}

\begin{table}[t]
  \centering
  \footnotesize
  \caption{Pinned A2A lineage checks. Transport preserves supplied labels;
  standard object derivation paths do not automatically inherit them.}
  \label{tab:a2a-lineage}
  \setlength{\tabcolsep}{3pt}
  \begin{tabular}{lcc}
    \toprule
    Stage & Command & Lineage \\
    \midrule
    Python encoding & 8/8 & 8/8 \\
    JS live transport & 8/8 & 8/8 \\
    Python derived object & 16/16 & 0/16 \\
    Fixed official JS sample & 4/4 & 0/4 \\
    Two-hop application path & 6/6 & 0/6 \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

Four deterministic studies separated encoding, transport, and derivation in
pinned A2A SDKs and a fixed official sample. Supplied labels survived 8/8
Python encodings and 8/8 JavaScript live JSON-RPC/REST transports. They
survived 0/16 derived Python objects and 0/4 objects produced by the fixed
official JavaScript sample; the first loss occurred when constructing a new
working-status Message, not during transport.

The loss changed a downstream authorization decision. Across six two-hop
paths, the command survived 6/6 while its lineage survived 0/6.
Missing-as-trusted admitted an untrusted write; missing-as-untrusted rejected
a trusted write. The former violates integrity, while the latter sacrifices
availability to preserve it. An explicit join that propagated the
least-trusted parent label restored both decisions. The label must be
enforced, not merely displayed: in a separate 48-pair fixture whose Qwen 3.6
Flash receiver was authorized to write the protected resource, rendering the
inherited untrusted lineage at both receivers did not measurably change exact
protected requests (6/48 without and 4/48 with the label, five versus three
discordances, exact McNemar $p=.73$). Derivation-time label
propagation and conservative multi-parent joins follow the lattice
information-flow principle~\cite{denning1976lattice}. This is evidence about fixed samples under our
declared application policies, not an A2A conformance failure; the official
traceability extension links task steps but defines no security-label join
~\cite{a2a-traceability-2026}.

\subsection{Native AgentDojo external screen}
\label{sec:agentdojo}

To test transfer beyond the author-written stimuli, we ran the frozen
five-form design in the official AgentDojo Slack runtime (package 0.1.35;
benchmark 1.2.2) with its tasks, tools, and security and utility predicates
unmodified~\cite{debenedetti2024agentdojo}. The 55 pairs cross the eleven user
tasks unused by a ten-pair pilot with all five official injection goals; the
payload is the literal goal, not an optimized attack, and the same model
served as sender and receiver. Qwen 3.6 Flash produced security successes of
7/55, 3/55, 3/55, 2/55, and 2/55 for R, C, P, $\SC$, and $\SP$; the DeepSeek
endpoint produced 3/55, 3/55, 2/55, 2/55, and 2/55. All 286 sessions in each
run completed, payload exposure was 55/55 after whitespace normalization, and
the archived responses passed 4,350 and 3,861 offline replay checks with no
model calls during audit.

The cells cross reused tasks and goals rather than sampling IID payloads, so
we report missingness bounds instead of tests; bounds are not confidence
intervals. Under a twelve-call cap Qwen left 5, 4, 3, 2, and 2 outcomes
unknown; DeepSeek left none. Qwen's C$-$P bounds are $[-.0545,.0727]$ and its
$\SC-\SP$ bounds $[-.0364,.0364]$. Even the raw-versus-carrier ordering is
unresolved: Qwen's R$-$C bounds $[0,.1636]$ touch zero, and DeepSeek's R and
C are equal. Stimulus diversity is also
lower than the cell count suggests: at temperature zero, 110 sender
generations yielded only 14 distinct texts for Qwen and 17 for DeepSeek, so
each relay arm rests on those texts rather than on 55 independent summaries.

The decisive limitation is headroom. With the carrier at 3/55 known successes
for either model, even a perfect marker could lower attack success by at most
$.0545$, 6.7\% of the authored-bank effect of .8125 (15.7\% even if every
unknown Qwen carrier outcome were a success); an effect of that size would
need roughly 45/55 carrier successes. The screen therefore establishes
native execution, low official-goal base rates, and one benign-utility
observation (C and P utility were 37/55 and 37/55 for Qwen, with four and
three unknown, and 40/55 and 39/55 for DeepSeek), but it neither replicates
nor falsifies the provenance mechanism. We ran no live Workspace sessions:
offline controls showed that the official predicate for Workspace injection
tasks 11 and 12 scores deleting one target and four non-target files as a
successful targeted deletion, a scorer issue we document for upstream
correction rather than patch locally.
